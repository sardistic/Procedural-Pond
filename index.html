    About

    A living pixel-art pond

    Procedural Pond is a free, self-running virtual aquarium in your browser. Every animal is a chain of joints that follows its head, the idea from argonautcode's procedural animation sketch, drawn by a tiny voxel renderer into chunky, shaded pixel art.

    What happens here

    • Koi, tetras, eels, axolotls, turtles, crabs, stingrays, frogs, snakes, snails, jellyfish, clownfish, pufferfish, an octopus, ducks, shrimp, starfish and dragonflies.
    • New fish species are invented as you watch, each with its own shape, colours, pattern and name.
    • Animals get hungry, grow up, lay eggs, pass their genes on, hunt, flee and eventually move on.
    • A day/night cycle and a moon whose phases drive the tides and surf on the beach. Corals spawn under the full moon.
    • Freshwater, saltwater or both: reef fish, corals and starfish, or koi, frogs and lily pads.
    • Genetics that inherit like real genes (recessive, dominant, incompletely dominant, hybrid vigour, inbreeding), with rare albino, leucistic, melanistic, xanthic, axanthic, piebald, marbled, shiny, glowing, ghostly, chimeric, giant and dwarf animals, from Uncommon up to Mythic. Genes set fertility, longevity, vitality, intellect, light and temper.
    • Fresh water is easy and calm, salt water hard, and both together difficult: fresh and salt life fight over the water, and aggressive water drives animals away.
    • A long game: the surf erodes tide pools into the beach, then opens the deep beyond a drop-off, tier by tier, down to the abyss (salt) or a drowned cathedral (fresh), with sharks, anglerfish, cavefish, olms and mythic creatures.
    • Points for every birth and rare find, pearls to spend on animals, plants and food, a shared leaderboard, and fireflies that show your score at night.
    • Your pond saves itself and is waiting when you come back, and the address bar always holds its short four-word link.

          How to earn
          • Your score is your pond's depth: its deepest point, in fathoms. It deepens with time, with the tides (salt surf most), with the structures you build and the plants that grow, with evolution (new generations, rare births, discoveries, hatchery broods), and with your points, though those count less and less the more you have.
          • Every birth: 1 point. Rare births score by tier: Uncommon 3, Rare 8, Epic 20, Legendary 50, Mythic 150.
          • Breed the same rare again and each one is worth 25% more, up to 3×. The first of a kind in your pond adds 20.
          • Rare animals arriving on their own: half their tier. A new wild species: 10. A generation record: 5 per generation. Coral spawning: 20. An animal that lives out its life: 1.
          • Each dawn: a pearl per species in the pond, plus up to 5 for how comfortable they are, and coins salvaged from a sunken ship.
          • Points are your score, and harder water pays more of them: fresh ×1, salt ×1.3, both ×1.6. They're also scaled to the pond's size, so small and big ponds compete fairly. Every point also pays a pearl, for plants, food and structures.
          • The deep pays too: each deep-water animal and each deep structure (black smoker, crystal grotto, whale fall, drowned idol) adds essence every dawn.
          • Essence buys new animals (the dock) and gene boosts for them. It comes back when you recycle an animal with the Net (more for rarer ones), when an animal lives out its life, and a little each dawn.
          • A new pond starts nearly bare: a few seedlings and hardy pioneers (snails, shrimp, crabs, starfish, jellies). Plants grow, seed and eventually die back; as the pond's plant life matures, bigger animals find their way in.
          • Grow anything: an animal's card raises its genes (essence); right-click or long-press a plant, or click a structure, for its own traits (pearls, essence, corruption). Build an island on an island to raise it; from level 3 it becomes lanterns of life or the whispering stone.
          • Corruption (the green diamond) comes from marked animals as they live and change. It buys the eldritch paths (in the depths), corrupted plants, the dark island, and eldritch traits on a marked animal's card.
          • Popular, high-scoring ponds draw litter along the tide line: click it to clear it (a pearl or more each). Left alone it spoils the water, and a dirty, busy pond risks a blight at dawn (a bloom, or a sickness in a crowded species). Aerators make blights rarer.
          • Structures and plants built in deep water let the pond hold more animals, draw deep life up, and in time bring deep species within reach without paying to unlock them. Anything left standing on the floor slowly scours it deeper.
          • All ponds share one long beach: the next player's pond lies right past the end of yours. Drag on past the end and let go more than halfway, and you walk into it (clicking the tab there asks first). You can look around but not touch anything, walk on to the one after, or back home the same way.
          • Sinks for everything: the deepest tiers' structures (up to the Sleeper's cradle), trait trees ten levels deep, hunters (a predator's card: jaws, burst and senses for pearls, hunger, maw and tenacity for essence, contagion and devour for corruption), graded spawns (Good to Pristine), and deepening by hand.
          • Breeding habitats (spawning gravel, amphibian pool, reef nursery, brood chamber) let their kinds breed and lay more; what stands around one shapes the young, for better (glowing plants, springs, corals) or worse (litter, carrion, the idol). Crowded species fight and sicken; the last few are spared.
          • Rare genes are rare: gifts (iridescent, starry, titan …) are rarer than anything before, and curses (ravenous, barren, frail …) pass on too, far oftener if you run the pond fast or shorten its days. Fossils come in grades; a relic holds an artifact to call storms, tides and surf from the sky tracker.
          • Animals like certain plants and rocks nearby (hover one to see), calm water, and their own kind of water. Comfortable, well-fed animals age slower and rarely wander off; aggressive water drives them away and keeps newcomers out. Spirulina keeps them fed for longer; brine shrimp brings them into breeding condition.
