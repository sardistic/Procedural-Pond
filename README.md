# Procedural Pond

A live top-down aquarium of procedurally animated animals, drawn in a chunky
voxel / pixel-art style. It is inspired by
[argonautcode/animal-proc-anim](https://github.com/argonautcode/animal-proc-anim).

**Live at [pond.nz](https://pond.nz/).**

To run it locally, open `index.html` in a browser. There is no build step and no server needed.
The pixel font loads from Google Fonts and falls back to monospace when offline. Short links and the
leaderboard need the small API in `server/`; without it the pond runs as before and shares long links.

## Using it

The screen has seven parts. The **rail** down the left holds the menu button (☰), the actions you've pinned, and ▸, which opens **all actions** as icons in five kinds that each fold away: food, tools, plants and rocks, builds, and creatures (to spawn). Hover any of them and press **+** to pin it to the rail (**−** takes it off); until you pin anything, the rail shows what you use most. Anything newly available (as the pond deepens, or a species unlocked) wears a **NEW** badge until you point at it or use it, and ▸ counts them; the pond remembers what it has shown you. A one-of-a-kind build (the hatchery, the idol, the Cyclopean gate, the Sleeper's cradle) already in the pond is greyed out and marked *built*. The **menu** (☰) holds your ponds, the habitat, scene options and buttons; it and the actions panel take turns on the left. Windows (an animal's or a structure's card, the hatchery, the score, the journal, the census, the depths) open one at a time: opening one closes the last, a click out in the pond closes it, and each keeps its title and × at the top as it scrolls. The **guide** (the rail's **?**, *Guide* in the menu, or **G**) covers everything in the game in sixteen chapters: every species (with a line about it, its water, stage, lifespan, how it breeds, what it likes, how to unlock it, how many are in the pond and a button to find one), plants and rocks, food and tools, builds, breeding and the hatchery, genes and traits, the depths, the sky, blood and the dark, the eldritch, the alien, hunters, the score and ledger, ponds and friends, and the controls. It's written from the game's own tables, so it keeps up; search looks through all of it, and *This pond* keeps to what this pond's water and depth can have.
The **animal dock** (bottom-centre) has a pixel icon per species, the most valuable first: a purple glow shows
what each species in the pond is worth (what recycling them would return), and a border and gem show its
rarest member's tier; the tooltip adds how many kinds of look it shows and its genetic diversity. Click one for
its **spawn card** (buy it with essence, with optional gene boosts, or recycle them all), and hover it for the species' **family tree**: every generation that has lived here, with lines to the parents,
their colours and rarity, and the animals themselves (those here now first, by points). Its
counter opens the **census**: every species with young, adult and elder counts, hunger, rares and the
highest generation. Click a species to list its members, and click a member to follow it. The census
also lists the wild species discovered. The **journal** (bottom-left) shows the latest event on one
line; click it (or press **J**) for the full log with filters. The **sky tracker** (top-right) is an
icon for the time of day (sun, dawn, dusk, or the moon in its current phase at night). Click it for
the day, moon phase, tide and surf, plus the light mode, day length, current and speed. The **pond
bar** (top-centre) shows the pond's name, its depth in fathoms (⚓, your score), pearls, essence and leaderboard
place (and corruption, once the marked have yielded some); click it (or press **P**) for what deepened the pond,
visitors, litter and the risk of a blight, what you earned lately, the leaderboard, and rare finds from every pond.
**Right-click or long-press** a plant (or click a structure) for its card: what it is, and its traits to grow.

- **Animals:** koi, tetra schools, eels, axolotls, turtles, crabs, stingrays, frogs, water snakes, snails, jellyfish, clownfish (each pair adopts an anemone and chases off intruders), pufferfish (inflate when threatened), octopus (FABRIK arms, camouflage, jets away in ink), duck families (ducklings follow in a line), shrimp (flick backwards when startled) and dragonflies. Fireflies come out on their own after dark. Each button adds one at a random spot.
  - *The bestiary:* 58 more species across every stage, each unlocked in the depths (the reef's and the pond's own for a little essence, the deep's for more) and arriving on their own now and then once their stage is open. The reef and the pond: mandarinfish, blue tangs, lionfish, seahorses, moray eels, hermit crabs, cleaner wrasse, nudibranchs; goldfish, guppies, bettas, clown loaches, crayfish, crested newts, plecos, pike. The twilight zone and deep lake: lanternfish, hatchetfish, comb jellies; sturgeon, burbot, paddlefish. The midnight zone and sunless cave: viperfish, black dragonfish, oarfish; cave crabs, glassfish, cave salamanders. The abyss and drowned cathedral: barreleyes, tripodfish, sea spiders; wraith carp, bell jellies, choirfish. The hadal trench and flooded crypts: giant amphipods, cusk-eels; crypt crabs, shroudfish. The black below and roots of the world: firefly squid, black swallowers; root crawlers, lamp eels. The drowned and sunken city: gargoyle fish, lantern jellies; bell wardens, runefish. The dreaming dark: the Dreamer and thoughtfish. The deep past: opabinia, helicoprion, arandaspis, tiktaalik. And where it stops being of this world: glass eels, void mantas, the lattice; starfins, hollow walkers, mirrorfish. The reef's and the pond's own breed anywhere; the deep's breed in the brood chamber. With many unlocked, the dock keeps to two rows and scrolls.
- **The pointer:** the default, first on the rail (an arrow): it looks. Click an animal, plant or structure for its card; the water gets nothing. Pick a food, a plant, a build or the Net to use it instead; **Esc** brings the pointer back.
- **Tools:** *Pellets* are free food. *Spirulina* (3 pearls) keeps animals well fed five times longer, and *Brine* shrimp (5) is a big meal that brings them into breeding condition. *Net* removes whatever animal, plant or rock you click. Every other tool places that plant or object where you click, for a few pearls: weed, eelgrass, anemone, coral, urchin, marimo moss ball, duckweed, lily pad or rock. Each is liked by certain species (the hint says which). Dragging an animal always leads it by the head.
- **World and view:** by default the pond is sized from your screen at 2× pixels (half the window in each direction), so at 2× the whole pond exactly fills the window. You start one step closer (3×), so animals swim in and out of view. You can't zoom out past a full screen, so there is never an empty border. Scene → World also offers fixed Small (720×405), Medium (960×540) and Large (1280×720) ponds. Share links carry the pond size, so a friend gets the identical pond. Pan by dragging empty water, with the arrow keys or WASD, or by clicking the minimap in the bottom-right. Only the visible part is rendered each frame.
- **Habitat:** *Fresh*, *Both* or *Salt*, under the pond name. Each habitat has its own species, plants, and default floor and water.
  - Fresh: koi, tetras, frogs, ducks, axolotls, turtles, lily pads, duckweed, marimo.
  - Salt: reef wild fish, clownfish in anemones, corals, urchins, starfish, jellies, octopus, rays.
  - Both: everything together.
- **Scene:**
  - floor: sand, coral sand, pebble bed, river stones, leaf litter or pool tiles. The pebbles, stones, leaves, shells and twigs are small 3D objects drawn by the same renderer as the animals.
  - water: teal, pond green, clear lagoon, tropical reef, deep blue or murky swamp. Water is more than a tint: depth fog makes the floor fade into the water colour while things near the surface stay crisp, the floor shimmers with refraction, particles drift in the current, and sun glints flash on the surface. Each preset has its own clarity.
  - light (in the sky tracker): a day/night cycle by default, or fixed day, dusk or night. **L** steps through the modes.
  - world size
  - current strength and sim speed
  - caustics, shadows and outlines on or off

  Settings are saved in localStorage.
- **Zoom:** scroll wheel or pinch, the +/− buttons (in a column beside the minimap), or the **+**, **−** and **0** keys. The zoom level is a whole number of screen pixels per pond pixel (the label shows 2×, 3× and so on), so the art stays crisp.
- **Your ponds:** every visitor gets their own pond, and it saves itself in the browser every few seconds (and when you leave). That covers the animals with their genes, names, ages and lineages, rares, eggs, discovered species, the journal, placed plants and rocks, and the day, moon and tide. Come back and it resumes where you left off. *Your ponds* in the menu lists your ponds (up to 12) to switch between or delete. *New pond* starts another pond while keeping this one. *Visitors can only look* makes the pond's link open for watching only: nobody can take a copy of it to grow as their own.
- **Signing in (Discord):** *Sign in with Discord* in *Your ponds* keeps your ponds in your account (the pond server stores which ponds are yours; Discord only tells it your name). Sign in on another browser and *Your ponds* lists them all; open one there and it is your own pond, not a copy, taken as far as whichever browser last played it, and it keeps itself in sync from there without its key. Ponds kept in an account are never cleared for going unused. *Show my name on this pond* puts your Discord name on it for others: on the leaderboard, on the beach as they walk up to it, and in the bar while they watch it. It's off until you tick it, per pond.
- **Short links:** every pond gets a link of four words a few seconds after it opens, like `pond.nz/amber-heron-moss-lantern`, and the address bar always shows it: that is the link *Share* / *Copy link* copies. The whole pond is stored behind it (animals, genes, family trees, journal, score) and kept up to date every minute and a half while you play, so the link always shows your pond as it is. Only the browser that made the link can update it; whoever else opens it gets their own copy, with a fresh score and a link of its own. Ponds are listed on the leaderboard from 50 points (untick *Show my pond on the leaderboard* to stay off it). Links nobody opens or updates are cleared after 45 days for ponds under 50 points, and after half a year for the rest.
- **Without the server:** until the short link arrives, or if the server can't be reached, the address bar holds just the pond's name and settings (`?pond=misty-reed-42`), which regrows that pond from day 1 anywhere and resumes it in your browser. Only then does *Share* fall back to a long link with the whole pond packed into its `#fragment`. Opened from a file, the address bar keeps that long link. If you open an older link to your own pond, your newer save wins. If you open someone else's copy of a pond you also have, you're asked before yours is replaced.
- **Seeds:** every pond has a seed name such as `misty-reed-42`, shown under the title. A plain `?pond=misty-reed-42` link (without the fragment) opens that pond from day 1, or resumes it if it's one of yours. *New pond* rolls a new seed.
- **Journal:** a running story of the pond: hatchings, arrivals, new species, who caught whom, dawn and dusk, rain. The newest entry pops up at the bottom-left, up to three lines of it, and the big news (rares, discoveries, the pond deepening) also shows in a banner under the pond bar. Click an entry to follow the animal it mentions.
- **The narrator:** someone is keeping notes on the pond: each new kind of life, what rises from the deep, the pond deepening, fossils and relics, what can be built now, the marked, the finest of each line. Their lines show under the pond bar and in the journal (Story). Their voice drifts, slowly, from a calm naturalist's field notes toward something much worse as the pond deepens, corruption gathers, the marked spread and the pond is kept in the dark; daylight brings some of it back. The score panel says how far gone they are.
- **Follow and Tour:** double-click an animal (or hover it and press **F**) to ride along with it. *Tour* (**T**) lets the camera wander between whatever is interesting. Esc or dragging stops it.
- **Sound:** *Sound* (**M**) turns on a generative soundscape, synthesized live with no audio files, that follows where you're looking. Beds mix by what's on screen: open water (brighter with the current, duller over the deep), surf that swells with each wave and lapping with the tide by the beach, wind with the gusts, rain, and a low drone over deep water that turns strange near transcendent things. Voices come from where they happen, panned across the view and fading off screen: plops and soft bubbles, frogs at night, ducks, gulls crying over the beach, crickets on the shore at night, birdsong by day near land, deep groans, whispers, and a struck note when one of the marked changes.
- **The shared beach:** every pond lies on one long beach with the others, in the order they were made. Past either end of your beach lies the next pond along, drawn right there beside yours at the same zoom, turned so its beach runs on from yours; the little arrows either side of your pond's name at the top say who's there (the score panel lists them too). Panning runs into the edge of your pond and stops there. Drag again from the end and the camera runs on over the next pond; let go and it stays where you left it (no spring back), and once the middle of the screen is over the next pond you're in it, at the same pixel size, with your own pond waiting behind you (nothing on screen jumps). You can take hold of either pond's picture to walk on or back. (Clicking an arrow asks first, then walks you over.) It's someone else's pond, run from its owner's latest save (the server's master copy) and brought up to date as they play. You can look around, follow animals and read cards, but nothing there can be touched or saved. Walk on past its far end for the one after, or back the way you came (or press *Return to my pond*) to come home.
- **Build:** big structures bought with pearls (the island and hatchery also take essence), each with an area of effect. Each arrives in its own way: an island rises out of the sea in a boil of bubbles, a ship falls from the surface and settles into the sand, and the monuments of the deep rise slowly, then all at once. New animals arrive in a ring of bubbles (the mythic make the whole pond scatter). Every tool, plant, food and build has a pixel icon drawn by the pond's own renderer.
  - *Sunken ship:* shelter that calms the water; salvaged coins each dawn. The deeper it goes down, the bigger the ship (a sloop in the shallows, a great liner over the abyss), the dearer (up to five times), and the more there is to salvage.
  - *Oil rig* (from the midnight zone, over deep water): a rich dawn in pearls and essence, a flare lit night and day, and the water around it unsettled and aggressive. It leaks: slicks drift and spread, sour and sicken what swims under them and wither plants (click a slick to skim it off). Where oil gathers thick, the tar may wake: a crawling thing of oil that swallows slicks to grow, withers what grows and poisons (and marks) what swims near. It starves without oil.
  - *Island:* dry land with a palm, where amphibians bask and nest; the water around it is sheltered. It sits low, so high tides cover its rim and low tides bare a wide beach, and it swells and shrinks over weeks. Out over the deep an island costs far more (up to six times, and so does raising it) and stands as a cliff, flat-topped and ringed with dark rock. Build another island on it to raise it, up to ten levels, each dearer than the last: every level adds a terrace, so it becomes a stepped mound, bigger, higher and lusher, with more palms and flowers. From level 3 it can go one of two ways, growing a step for each level above the second (up to six): *lanterns of life* (little lights and fireflies; animals nearby are comforted and breed more) or *the whispering stone* (a black stone on the summit that yields corruption and drives nearby animals mad; young born near it may wake touched).
  - *Thermal vent* (salt) or *spring* (fresh): warm or clear water that makes animals fertile and feeds plankton.
  - *Glow shrine:* a crystal that lights the night.
  - *Aerator:* oxygen that slows ageing.
  - *Seed bed:* plants take root around it.
  - *Hatchery:* an idle breeding game. Stock it with breeding pairs, one pair to a pen: while a place is empty it lists your pond's breeding lines (any kind with two grown animals), most valuable first with their traits, and one click puts a pair in (or use the creature card's *To hatchery*). It starts with one pen; *Breeding pens* adds up to four more, and the pairs take turns. Feed it by clicking, or with an auto-feeder that keeps going while you're away. Each brood leans toward the trait you pick (size, speed, fertility, longevity, vitality, intellect, light, tolerance, calm or rarity, and once the pond is deep, *the deep dream*), and the best of the brood takes the weaker parent's place in its pen, so each line is bred forward a generation at a time. It adds variety, not a horde: a pair rests while its kind already makes up 8% of what the pond can hold (at least 10). An ancient gene from a fossil can be infused into the next brood. Upgrades, many small steps each: bigger scoop, auto-feeder, bigger tank, incubator, UV lamp, selective filter and breeding pens. Click the hatchery in the pond, or the egg in the dock (it fills as the next brood's food comes in), to open it again.

  - *Breeding habitats:* spawning gravel (koi, tetras, wild fish, eels, rays, pufferfish), an amphibian pool (frogs, axolotls, snails, turtles, crabs), a reef nursery (salt: clownfish, shrimp, starfish, jellies, octopus, crabs) and, from the midnight zone, a brood chamber for the deep's own kind. Animals breed at their habitat and lay half as many again; eels, rays, pufferfish, turtles, crabs, starfish, jellies, octopus and the deep species breed nowhere else. What stands around a habitat shapes the young born there, for better or worse: glowing plants or a shrine (glow), springs, aerators and root cathedrals (hardier), vents and smokers (bigger), an island of life (longer-lived), corals and anemones (luckier), the idol, the gate or a dark island (the mark), litter (sickly or frail young), carrion (rotting). A habitat's card lists who breeds there and what is shaping them.

  Over days, the ground around structures, rocks and placed plants discolours (algae in fresh water, coralline pink in salt), plants take root around them, and the floor around them scours deeper, reaching toward the deep. Click a structure for its card: *reach* and *strength* upgrades (pearls) widen and strengthen its effect. Built in deep water, structures (and deep plants) let the pond hold more animals, draw deep life up more often, and in time bring deep species within reach without paying to unlock them.
- **Census:** the dock's counter lists species by worth, with their genetic diversity; open one to see its animals (most valuable first) and to recycle them all.
- **Creature card:** click an animal (or follow or tour) for everything about it: its genes and what they do, its genotype, inbreeding and hybrid vigour, its family tree from grandparents to young, its story from the journal, and buttons to follow or recycle it. Rarity tiers, traits, genes, waters and currencies have one colour each wherever they're mentioned.
- **The depths:** over a long game, the pond wears deeper (salt water fastest, fresh slowest). The tides do most of it, with time, your structures, the plant life, and evolution: new generations, rare births (firsts most), new species, deep sightings, fossils, broods and transcendence. Points count too, on a log curve, so points can't run away with depth. The score panel shows what deepened it and when the next tier should open. A salt pond reaches its tide pools in about 20 to 40 minutes at the default speed, twilight within an hour or two, midnight in a few hours and the abyss after many; fresh water takes about three times as long. First tide pools are scoured into the beach, cut off at low tide. Then the far side falls away past a ragged drop-off, and the world grows that way, one darker band per tier: the twilight zone, the midnight zone and the abyss in salt water, or a deep lake, a sunless cave and a drowned cathedral in fresh. Deep water swallows the light (only things that glow stay bright). The side view above the minimap shows the pond cut from the beach to the deepest water, with each animal at its depth and the erosion toward the next tier; click it for the evolution tree, where each tier's species can be unlocked for essence (and the pond worn deeper faster).
  - *Salt:* sand sharks in the shallows and tide pools, reef sharks, then anglerfish with glowing lures, gulper eels, vampire squid and giant isopods, and in the abyss the Kraken and the Leviathan.
  - *Fresh:* giant catfish in the deep lake, blind cavefish, olms and giant isopods in the sunless cave, and the Watcher in the drowned cathedral.
  - Each tier also opens structures to build, foods and plants. Twilight / deep lake: kelp forest or drowned forest (shelter and a nursery), krill or bloodworms. Midnight / sunless cave: black smoker or crystal grotto (warmth or light, essence each dawn), black coral or glowcaps (they grow only in deep water), marine snow. Abyss / drowned cathedral: a whale fall (a feast for deep life) or the drowned idol (it draws the mythic up, but nothing near it rests easy).
  - Past the abyss, four more tiers take the pond much further out and deeper, each stranger than the last:
    - *The hadal trench* (salt: snailfish, frilled sharks, brine pools, tube worms) or *the flooded crypts* (fresh: crypt eels, the ossuary, pale roots); chum.
    - *The black below* or *the roots of the world*: siphonophores (lit necklaces, drifting) and giant squid; the spire of teeth or the root cathedral; lure lanterns; sea lilies or weeping moss.
    - *The drowned city* or *the sunken city*: the Deep Ones, who walk the streets; the Cyclopean gate (unique, and vast); star-weed; offerings for the marked.
    - *The dreaming dark*: the Sleeper's cradle (unique, and very dear), and the Sleeper itself.
    - *The deep past* (from erosion 300): a gene pool of what should be long gone. *The Cambrian sea* (trilobites, anomalocaris, ammonites) or *the Carboniferous swamps* (sea scorpions, lungfish); *the Devonian dark* (dunkleosteus) or *the Devonian lakes* (placoderms, giant amphibians), with the coelacanth in both; *the Permian deep* (plesiosaurs, mosasaurs) or *the primordial mire* (Hyneria).
    - *Where it stops being of this world* (from erosion 1,010): *the Starfall trench* or *the Fallen-star mire*, *the Glass garden* or *the Grey fen*, *the Other sea* or *the Other water*. Alien artifacts land oftener the deeper it goes (see *Alien artifacts and parasites*).
    - *And on:* past those the tiers are made up as the pond reaches them, each half again as far as the last, each wearing the pond further out and darker. Between tiers the pond keeps reaching further out, a little every dawn (faster the deeper it has gone and the more it has scored), up to 15,000 px of deep in all (a pond that's very wide across reaches less far, and a phone less far again, so the pond always fits in memory). Growing keeps your zoom and whatever you were following; on a big pond the part on screen is redrawn at once and the rest a little at a time.
  - Deep life also turns up by itself, and each deep animal adds essence at dawn. Mythic animals are vast and rare; while one is in the pond, strange things are noted in the journal.
  - *The deep looks deeper as you go:* the dark shifts colour with each tier past the abyss (indigo, violet, the green of the drowned city, the red-black of the dream in salt water; crypt brown, root black, sunken green and dream red in fresh), past the dreaming dark it goes darker with every tier, marine snow starts to glow from the midnight zone, and from the trench on, a long canyon opens across the deep (a second from the drowned city): a black floor far down, walls stepping down into it, and a lip lit on one side where things glint.
  - *Light:* glowing things cast pools of neon light on the floor and whatever swims through: glowing fish, the deep's own lamps (the angler's lure throws a long beam ahead of it, squid and gulpers shorter ones), paragons, the transcendent, glowing and deep plants, and the deep's structures (lure lanterns, shrines, grottoes, smokers, the gate, the cradle). Salt water glows cyan, blue and magenta; fresh water foxfire green, amber and violet. The pools brighten what's under them, in the light's colour, and show at night and (more faintly) over the deep by day; a crowd glows like a few lamps, not a hundred.
  - *Up and down:* out over the deep, swimmers rise toward the surface at night and sink by day, schools together; the big hunters and monsters surge up and dive again; the kraken and the squids lift off the floor and rise. The dark covers what swims low and lets go of what comes up.
  - *The floor takes shape:* rocks and structures scour the floor around them into hollows that trail away toward the deep (deeper tiers scour harder), while the ground right under them is sturdier and stands proud. Plants don't dig: their beds trap sand.
- **Map layers:** the button on the minimap switches between the pond, *Tension* (how aggressive the water is) and, with both waters, *Water* (where it runs fresh or salt). The minimap shows the beach as the tide bares it.
- **Tooltips:** point at almost anything for a little pixel-art tooltip saying what it does: every button, tool and build (with its price), the pond bar, the dock, the spawn card, the sky, and in the pond itself plants, rocks, structures, fossils, litter, remains, eggs and trenches (animals have their own hover card).
- **Big crowds:** when more than about 140 animals are on screen, the crowd is drawn coarser (no small fins, fewer tentacles; what you follow or point at stays whole), and if frames still run slow, the costliest surface touches (sky glints, murk, chop, then light pools and caustics) step down until there's room again.
- **Keys:** **B** shows the bones (spine chains, joint radii, leg IK), **Space** pauses, **L** switches light modes, **F** follows, **T** tours, **M** toggles sound, **N** music, **P** opens the score, **G** opens the guide (Esc closes it), **H** hides the panel. *Photo* saves a PNG.

## Depth, points, pearls and the leaderboard

- **Depth** is your score: the pond's deepest point in fathoms (a few for a new pond, hundreds in the twilight zone, thousands in the abyss, tens of thousands in the trench, and hundreds of thousands in the dreaming dark; fresh water about a third as deep). The leaderboard ranks ponds by it.
- **Points** never go down; harder water pays more (fresh ×1, salt ×1.3, both ×1.6), and points scale with the square root of a 960×540 reference over the pond's area (×0.6 to ×1.5), so small and large ponds compete fairly. Every point also pays a **pearl**, which you spend on plants and rocks (2 to 15) and special food. A new pond starts with 30 pearls.
- **Essence** (◆) buys new animals. A spawn's price follows the species' size, rarity, how reliably it settles in (the ones that don't return half) and how long it lives. Gene boosts (fertile, long-lived, hardy, clever, bright, calm, adaptable, or a hidden rare carrier gene) cost extra, and so does quality: pay for Good, Fine, Superb or Pristine (×1.6 up to ×30) and every animal in the spawn is at least that good. Essence comes back when you recycle an animal with the Net or from its card (more for grown and rarer ones), when animals live out their lives, from remains and fossils, and a little each dawn. A new pond starts with 15.
- **Earning:** every birth is a point. Rare births score by tier: Uncommon 3, Rare 8, Epic 20, Legendary 50, Mythic 150. Breeding the same rare again pays 25% more each time, up to 3×, and the first of a kind in your pond adds 20. Rares that arrive on their own score half. A new wild species is 10, a generation record 5 per generation, coral spawning 20, and an animal that lives out its life 1. Each dawn pays a pearl per species in the pond plus up to 5 for how comfortable everyone is.
- **Investing:** whatever you spend on something lasting is an investment: building and upgrading structures, raising islands, plants and rocks, spawning animals, growing their traits and hunting skills, unlocking deep species, deepening the pond, and the eldritch paths and the dark's offerings (food doesn't count). A tenth comes straight back as points (and pearls), and each dawn the ledger pays a dividend of points and essence: 4% a day for building, 5% for planting and evolving, 3% for new life, 4% for deepening, 4.5% for the dark (in pearls' worth: essence counts ×5, corruption ×8). What's in animals wears down as they age and go; a structure stays on the ledger until it's taken down. Reaching each new depth tier pays 150 × tier² points. The score panel shows what's invested and what it pays.
- **Corruption** (the green diamond) comes from marked animals as they live, change and take in their own, and from some deep structures and hunters. It buys the eldritch paths (in the depths), corrupted plants, the whispering stone, the eldritch traits on a marked animal's card, hunters' contagion and devour, and the Sleeper's cradle.
- **Somewhere to put it all:** the deep's structures (up to 100,000 pearls for the cradle), trait trees that go ten levels deep (each dearer than the last), hunters, graded spawns, and deepening the pond by hand, which costs a little more each time.
- **Hunters:** any predator's card (and, for 50 essence and 25 corruption, any swimmer's: *wake it to the hunt*) builds it up in eight ways, ten levels each: jaws, burst and senses (pearls), hunger, maw and tenacity (essence), and contagion and devour (corruption). Built up far enough a hunter takes anything smaller than itself; with devour each kill grows it and yields corruption, and with contagion its kills spread the mark (if it carries it) or the rot to those nearby.
- **Litter and blights:** the more visitors a pond has (the server counts them) and the higher its score, the more litter washes up along the tide line: bottles, cans, bags, ghost nets, tyres, leaking drums. Litter spoils the water around it (comfort, plant growth), ghost nets snare small animals, and a dirty, busy pond risks a blight at dawn: an algal bloom (a red tide in salt water) that chokes weak animals, or a sickness in the most crowded species. Click litter (with any tool) to haul it out for a pearl or more; aerators make blights rarer. The score panel shows visitors, litter and the risk.
- **Light and the dark:** darkness feeds madness and daylight resists it. The change in the marked, fits of madness, the madness itself, madness quirks and the dreams all run faster in the dark (right now, and on average over the last day and a half) and slower in the light, while the dark yields more corruption. A pond kept always in daylight resists corruption; one kept always at night gives in to it. The score panel shows the balance.
- **Alien artifacts and parasites:** from the deep past on (rarely at first, oftener the deeper the pond goes, and sometimes with a meteor shower), something not of this world comes to rest on the deep floor at dawn: a magenta *hooked ring*, a cyan *spiral horn* or a lime *glass bloom*. Each carries a strain of parasite and adapts: most dawns its strain grows a generation older, and the older it is the more it releases (more at night). The Net breaks one up for points and essence, but a shard often lingers and grows back.
  - *The parasites*, each its own colour and shape: the **latcher** (a magenta hooked star), the **coilworm** (a cyan worm) and the **sporeling** (a lime cluster). A larva drifts toward the nearest animal it can ride and latches on (no new hosts are taken once about a quarter of the pond is ridden). Riding its host it feeds on it and changes it (*latched*: quick to anger; *coiled*: glowing but shorter-lived; *sporing*: breeds more, sickens), breeds more larvae, and spreads its contagion. When its host dies or leaves, it jumps off; when its host is eaten, it goes into whatever ate it. A strain grows older as it passes on.
  - *Contagions*: **glassing** (the latcher's: the skin crystallizes, hard but short-lived; passes to its kind by touch), **xenofever** (the coilworm's: restless, it may leave; passes to its kind) and **the spore** (the sporeling's: slow and sickening; passes to any kind by touch, and can kill the weak). Dawn clears some.
  - *The evolved*: very rarely (one chance a dawn for the whole pond, better with an older strain), an animal that has carried a third-generation or older strain for a day gives rise to something new: a young of its kind with one of its parasite's traits in its blood for good: **chitinous** or **frenzied** (latcher), **luminous** or **long-coiled** (coilworm), **bloomborn** or **spore-bearing** (sporeling). It's Mythic, marked in its trait's colour, immune to its own parasite, and passes the trait on (usually, with two evolved parents).
  - *The quarantine lamp* (a build, from the Devonian on): in its violet light parasites let go and don't latch on, spread or breed, and larvae die.
- **The land remembers:** over the days the floor takes on the character of what lives, dies, grows and stands on it, and slowly the landscape changes to match. A coarse memory of the floor holds eight things: bone, life, light, the dark, madness, the alien, the cryptid (the great things that pass) and the ancient. Animals leave their nature where they spend their time (the thriving, the marked, the mad, the parasite-ridden, the mythic, the prehistoric, the glowing); skeletons sink into the sand and enrich it by what they were (some stay, half sunk, and sink over about twelve days); plants add to it as they grow and as they die; structures, artifacts, blood, oil and fossils mark it too. Each dawn it fades a little and spreads a little.
  - *What it becomes:* **bone sand** (plants grow faster; scavengers gather), **the green** (it seeds itself with plants, and animals are content), **glow sand** (it glimmers and calms the water), **the ashen floor** (plants wither and the mark comes on faster), **red silt** (the water there is quick to anger), **the glass** (parasites breed faster), **the watched deep** (the pond wears deeper; the mythic come oftener) and **fossil beds** (fossils work loose from them, in the water). Each tints and textures the floor in patches, repainted a little at a time, and the map's *Land* layer shows it all.
  - *Formations:* where it holds strongly the land grows its own features over the days, and loses them if it fades: bone reefs, hummocks or coral heads, glow crystals and glass spires (both shine at night), black shards, red spurs, great footprints and stromatolites.
  - *Islands:* each has its own character from its seed (a palm cay, a coral cay, a rocky skerry, a reed isle, a willow islet, or a black islet over the deep) and its own plants, which sprout, grow, die (standing dead for a few days) and come back, leaning toward what the land around it has become: blooms that glow, black moss and dead wood, red thorns, glass shoots, cycads and horsetails, bleached bones. Two islands close together grow a sandbar over about ten days (under water at first, then dry) and join; what they become together depends on what each has become: a lagoon atoll, the black causeway, a bridge of glass, a fossil ridge, a bone bar, a lantern walk, a thorn bar, or twin cays. An island's card and tooltip say what it is and whom it has joined.
  - *Islands that grow:* an island keeps changing for as long as it stands. It grows up from bare sand through greening, wooded and forested to old growth (with time, what grows on it, height and the life in the land around it), each stage carrying more and bigger growth. Its size drifts toward what its water allows: sheltered water, a living shore, reed beds, mangroves and a reef build it out; surf wears it (salt water most, and hardest out over the deep); storms take bites out of it and it builds back slowly; an eruption adds new rock; and what grew where the sea took the ground dies back. Over its first forty days or so its coastline wanders into headlands and coves. Things arise on it of their own accord where they suit it (a reef round a coral cay, reeds round a fresh one, mangroves in salt shallows, birds nesting on a wooded island, tide pools in rock, a spring on a high forested one, fire under a black islet out over the deep, a great tree on old growth), and its card grows them further, besides raising it and its branch: **nourish the beach** (it grows by a tenth at once), **plant a grove**, a **fringing reef** (salt water; at the third level a **barrier reef** rises offshore over about ten days, its crest baring at low tide, with a calm lagoon inside) or **reed beds** (fresh), **mangroves** (salt shallows: a nursery, and the island stops washing away), a **rookery** (terns over salt water, herons over fresh: they fish the pond, and pay pearls and essence each dawn), **tide pools**, a **spring pool**, **the fire** (a vent: warm water, plankton, a glow at night, eruptions that grow it) and **the old giant**. Life comes to live on it: iguanas bask on its rocks (skinks in fresh water), seals haul out on the bigger salt islands, terns wheel and dive and herons stand in the shallows, and frogs, dragonflies and fireflies gather at a spring. They aren't the pond's: click one to hear what it is.
  - Everything moves slowly: formations, island plants and sinking bones ease from one dawn's state to the next through the day.
- **New kinds come slowly:** a new pond knows only its pioneers (snails, shrimp, crabs, starfish, jellies). Every other kind has to find its way in first, one kind at a time, with a pause after each that grows as the pond gets to know more (about a pond day and a half at first, several days later on), and a kind can be spawned from the dock only once it has come (the deep's own, and the reef's and pond's rarer kinds, can still be unlocked in the depths for essence). Each pond has its own odds for each kind, from its seed: some are common here, some rare, and the odds drift with the seasons, each kind on its own slow cycle. The dock's **?** opens *Wildlife*: every kind that could still come, how likely it is here, whether the pond is alive enough for it yet, and when the next new kind can come. A **lure** (pearls) makes a kind far likelier to be the next, for two days, and lets it come a little sooner; a **summons** (essence) brings it at the next dawn, whatever the odds. Older ponds count everything they've already had as known.
- **Animals together:** different kinds meet and do things, each with its own little dance and a sign over them. *Cleaning stations*: a cleaner wrasse or a shrimp swims up to a bigger fish, which holds still while the cleaner circles its head; it comes away calmer, and now and then the cleaner takes off a parasite or a sickness. *Displays*: territorial kinds (bettas, clownfish, crabs, hermit crabs, crayfish, lionfish, morays, pike) meeting something their size flare up and circle each other until one backs down. *Riders*: small fish ride alongside a big calm animal, and the hunters leave them be. *Bait balls*: a school swirls tight when a hunter comes close. *Warnings*: lionfish, pufferfish and sea spiders flare at a hunter coming for them, and it gives up. *Scraps*: scavengers trail a hunter and pick up after its catch.
- **The balance, by hand:** in the census, open a kind: **🛡 Protect** keeps the hunters off it (a pearl a dawn for every two; it lapses if you can't pay), and **🎯 Cull** sends them after it first, with a bounty for every catch. The census marks a kind that has outgrown the pond. The **refuge** (a build) is a thicket of stone arches and weed: nothing inside it can be caught.
- **The cycle:** dying plants, picked-over carcasses and old food that sinks uneaten leave detritus on the floor. Scavengers (crabs, snails, shrimp, isopods, catfish, starfish, hermit crabs, crayfish, loaches, plecos, amphipods, the cave and crypt crabs, sea spiders, trilobites, root crawlers) seek it out and eat it: it feeds them, and the rest goes back into the floor as richness, so plants grow better where they work (what nobody eats rots in, slower). Hermit crabs, octopuses and crayfish move into bottles and cans, and morays and eels into tyres (a home, and one less piece of litter); small scavengers pick ghost nets apart; an old tyre grows over into a little reef. The score panel keeps the tally.
- **Wanderers:** now and then a fierce animal that leaves one pond turns up in another along the beach: a hunter by nature (sharks and the deep's other hunters), one woken to the hunt or built up in it, the feral, the predatory and abominations, or the deeply marked. When one leaves a pond that has its link, it may join the server's pool (for a fortnight); at dawn, rarely (6%), a pond calls up one that can live in its water, never one of its own. It arrives red-edged, the natives scatter, and for a day or so it hunts and fights whatever the hour, keeping count of its kills, then moves on (and may go on to terrorize another pond). The journal says whose pond it came from.
- **Rage:** darkness and corruption make animals aggressive. At night, under a blood moon and in blood rain, hunters hunt sooner and further; the marked (changed or worse) turn on anything smaller than themselves in the dark, and pick fights with their equals: the weaker bleeds, and may be torn apart.
- **The dice:** as one of the marked changes, when it takes in its own, and sometimes on a red night, it rolls two dice and its nature warps: withered (2), nocturnal (3–4), in love with the dark (5–6: it likes carrion, idols, the deep things and poisoned water, not what its kind likes), feral (7), vast (8), a hunter (9), many-eyed (10), tireless (11), or on double six an abomination. Each is a trait on its card and changes what it does; the journal says what was rolled.
- **Blood in the water:** a red cloud where an animal is caught or hurt, drifting and fading. Hunters smell it from far off and come. A hunter closing in leaves a wake of bubbles, and its quarry bolts and jinks.
- **Paragons:** the finest animal a line of yours can produce. Breed a rare line again and again (the same traits in the same species: 3, 8, 20 and 50 times), or have a species score for you (150 points, then 600, 2,400 and so on), and it earns a Paragon: free, Pristine, carrying the line's traits and a gift, hardier and longer-lived, with a crown of light; it always settles, and its spawn come Superb. The rail's **♛** opens *Paragons*: what they are, the ones waiting (claim them there), and how close each line and species is to the next, with a count on the crown when any are waiting.
- **Keep safe:** mark any animal from its card (Keep safe) and nothing recycles it, not the Net and not "recycle all", until you unmark it. The census shows a lock by its name.
- **Remains and fossils:** an animal that dies of old age or hunger sinks and leaves its bones on the floor; click them (any tool but the Net) for extra essence and a few points before the scavengers (crabs, snails, shrimp, starfish, isopods, catfish) pick them clean. Now and then, at dawn, the tide uncovers a fossil on the beach. They come in grades, rarer in deeper ponds: an ammonite (common), a trilobite, a great tooth, a bead of amber with something inside, an ancient skull, and very rarely a relic. Click it for essence, points and an ancient gene (the rarer the fossil, the more of each, and the rarer the gene): albino, piebald, giant, shiny, glow, ghost, chimera, the eldritch mark and others. Give it to a new spawn (the spawn card) or a hatchery brood, and the animal shows it.
- **Artifacts:** a relic holds an artifact, a hand on the pond itself. From the sky tracker: the *storm glass* calls a storm or clears the sky for a day, the *moonstone* pulls the tides wide or stills them, the *tide bell* holds high or low water for half a day, and the *wind conch* raises the surf or lays it flat (each rests half a day after use). Others simply work: the *heart of pearl* (a quarter more pearls), the *lodestone of the deep* (the pond wears deeper half as fast again) and the *eye of the deep* (deep life rises twice as often).
- **Leaderboard:** the top twenty ponds by depth, each with its best find; click one to visit (you get your own copy). *Rare finds in every pond* lists the latest Rare-or-better animals born or arriving anywhere. The server only takes scores that grow at a plausible rate.
- **Fireflies keep score:** at night the swarm shows how your depth compares to the high-score range (tenth place on the leaderboard, or 110 fathoms while that is lower): a few fireflies for a new pond, a full swarm in the high-score range. Past it, **blue fireflies** join, two more each time your depth doubles, and many more for the top three.

## The living pond

With **Life** on (Scene section), the pond runs itself:

- **A slow start:** a new pond is nearly bare: a few seedlings and hardy pioneers (snails, shrimp, crabs, starfish, jellies), with little to spend. Plants grow from seedlings over a few pond days, seed around themselves each dawn, and die back when they're old (weeds and duckweed in weeks, corals and moss balls in months), so the pond fills in without overcrowding. As its plant life matures, the animals it's ready for find their way in, from tetras and dragonflies to octopus and snakes. Plants you place start as seedlings too.
- **Growing individuals:** an animal's card raises any of its working genes up to ten levels (essence, dearer each time). Built all the way, a trait shows on the animal, subtly and on the move: fertile, a rose glint rising off its back; long-lived, a silver shimmer running down its spine; hardy, bronze flecks along its flanks; clever, a blue spark circling its head; bright, shining eyes; calm, a faint ring spreading from it; adaptable, a back that shifts through colours. A hunter's skills do the same at their last level: pale fangs (jaws), a streak behind it (burst), amber eyes and a sweeping arc (senses), a red flicker in its eyes when hungry (hunger), a dark red gape as it closes in (maw), an ember at its tail (tenacity), sickly green motes (contagion), dark motes circling in (devour). Each button's tip says what it will look like. A plant's card (right-click or long-press it) makes it lush (bigger, more cover), hardy (lives longer, minds litter less) or seeding (spreads further) for pearls, glowing (little lights at night that comfort animals and draw plankton) for essence, or corrupted (black and wrong: it yields corruption, unsettles those near it and marks young born by it) for corruption.
- **The river:** a river runs down the beach from the landward edge (the same way every time the pond regrows): wide in fresh water, middling with both, a trickle in salt. It widens and cuts deeper as the pond ages and deepens, swells and shrinks with the wet and dry seasons, its bends grow and creep downstream, and every week or two of pond time it breaks its banks and cuts a new course to the sea while the old channel silts up. It stays wet at every tide, calms and (with both waters) freshens the water at its mouth, brings nutrients and seeds down, and fresh life likes to gather there. Its flow swings with the seasons, and now and then (from the pond's seed) a flood runs it high and wide for a day or two, or a drought takes it down to a trickle; the journal says so. Its channels run deepest down the middle and deeper the longer it keeps a course, and old courses linger as creeks for a while before they silt up.
- **Up the beach:** past the top of the beach the land carries on, and you can pan up onto it. At first only a little shows; the haze lifts further as the pond ages and deepens (the journal says what comes out of it). First the upper beach, with the weed, shells and driftwood the storms threw up; then dunes held by marram grass, pocked with ghost crabs' holes, the river cutting down between them; then scrub, palms (or willows and reeds by fresh water) and a heron fishing the river by day, fireflies over it at night; and at last a jungle, dark under its canopy and black further in, where eyes look out, a few by day and many at night, blinking, and slipping away when you come near. Nothing has come out of it yet. The river runs on up the land into the trees. It isn't part of the pond: click it to hear what it is.
- **Life on the beach:** animals of the beach itself (not the pond's: no genes, not in the census, not yours to net or carry off; click one to hear what it is). On some dark nights a **sea turtle** (salt water and both) comes in from the sea and heaves herself up the sand, leaving a track like a tyre's; above the high-water mark she digs, lays, covers the nest and goes back to the sea, and the tide washes her track away. Two or three pond days later, at dusk or dawn, the nest hatches and the **hatchlings** run for the water: gulls and ghost crabs take some, and each that makes it is worth an essence. Click a nest to see when it's due. **Ghost crabs** live in burrows high on the beach: out at dusk and through the night they dash and freeze, chase hatchlings, and run for their holes when anything comes near. By day a little flock of **sandpipers** runs the water's edge after the waves; come close and they take off low along the beach.
- **Sand:** the water moves sand around. Sandbars build offshore with the surf (salt water most) and creep along the beach, their crests showing at low tide; the river builds a delta of sand at its mouth (and when it moves, the old one wears away); a spit grows from the beach out to an island near it, until it joins it to the shore; and thick old plant beds trap sand, so the floor under them goes pale and ripple-marked. It all follows from the pond's age, so links regrow it exactly.
- **Gulls:** by day, gulls come to ponds with a beach (more over salt water). They soar over the water, land to walk the sand (taking off when the tide or your pointer comes close), scavenge remains, and now and then dive on a frog, a crab or a dragonfly out in the open. They leave at dusk and in heavy rain.
- **Waves and the sky:** out over the deep the swell shows as wave marks, little arcs that drift with it, each swelling up, breaking and fading, more and bolder in rough water. In the shallows a short chop runs across the swell when it blows; out over the deep in a storm, the wind tears streaks of spindrift along it. A swell rolls across the surface toward the beach, each wave lit on its face and shadowed behind (in dithered steps, like the rest of the art), broken up along its crests. In wind, surf and rain the waves run longer, faster and taller, much bigger out over the deep, and spray breaks off the tops of the biggest. Calm water shows the sky the way rippled water does, as short glints that drift and twinkle, crowding where the clouds overhead are bright (pale by day, warm at dawn and dusk, deep blue at night). The deep has a slow murk moving through it.
- **Natural habits:** each species keeps its own hours (crabs, eels, octopus and snails by night, rays at dusk, most fish by day) and rests out of hours near what it likes. Animals gather by the plants, rocks and structures they like, turtles haul out to bask on the beach by day, crabs forage the bared sand at night, sharks never stop swimming, and scavengers go to carrion.

- **Genes:** every animal has its own size, body length and girth, hue, saturation, brightness and speed. Babies blend their parents' genes with a small mutation, so lineages drift over generations. Occasionally a giant or a dwarf appears.
- **Rare morphs:** albino (white with red eyes), axanthic (blue-grey), xanthic (golden), melanistic (near-black) and piebald (white patches) are recessive. An animal can carry one hidden copy and pass it on, so two carriers can produce a rare baby. Shiny (a swapped palette and sparkles), glow (bioluminescent, bright at night) and ghost (pale, see-through colours) are rare mutations that often pass on. Rare animals get a thick outline in their trait's colour. The journal announces them, and the hover card shows their traits and what they carry.
- **Gifts and curses:** rarer still are the gifts: iridescent (a sheen through every colour), starry, vigorous, keen, swift, ancient and titan (vast for its kind). And the curses: ravenous (burns through food and starves), barren, frail, rotting, twitching, cannibal (hunts its own kind's young), feral, sickly (often fails to settle), stunted, addled (its eggs often fail) and brittle. Both pass to the young like any gene. Curses turn up out of nowhere only rarely, but far oftener in a pond run fast or with short days: time pushed too hard breeds wrongness.
- **Quality:** every animal is graded from its genes (its working genes above or below the average, gifts up, curses down): Poor, Fair, Good, Fine, Superb or Pristine. The grade shows on its card and in the census, and raises what it's worth.
- **Madness and sickness:** when one of the marked changes, sometimes something stays with it: whispering, many-mouthed, weeping (it leaks the mark), phasing, hungering, dreaming or hollow-eyed. Two ills go round: the rot (from carrion, fouled water and crowding; it ages its host fast and passes by touch within a kind) and the madness (from being driven mad over and over, or from the mad at night). Dawn cures some.
- **Keeping numbers in check:** a species crowded well past what the pond holds for it starts to fight over room (the weaker can die of it) and breeds the rot; a species down to its last few seeks itself out and breeds sooner, and blights and sickness spare the last three.
- **Rarity tiers:** each trait adds its rarity (piebald 1; giant, dwarf, melanistic and xanthic 2; albino and axanthic 3; shiny, glow, ghost and touched 4; changed 5; eldritch 7), and the sum sets the tier: Uncommon, Rare (2-3), Epic (4-5), Legendary (6-7) or Mythic (8+).
- **Difficulty:** fresh water is easy (calm, few predators), salt water hard (surf, big tides, a busier food chain), and both together difficult: the sea side of the pond runs salt and the far side fresh, like an estuary, and fresh and salt life each pull the water their way. Animals caught in the wrong water are uneasy and leave.
- **Aggression:** every part of the pond has a temper. Predators (hungry ones most), recent hunts, crowds and surf raise it; plant cover and calm, giant animals lower it. Aggressive water drives animals away, costs eggs, stops breeding and keeps newcomers out, who come in by the calmest edge.
- **Working genes:** besides colour and shape, every animal has fertility, longevity, vitality, intellect, light, aggression, tolerance (of the other water), territory and resilience. They blend and drift like real quantitative traits. Rare traits add buffs (glowing animals light their patch at night and draw plankton, ghosts are hard for predators to see, giants calm the water, dwarfs are fertile, chimeras cope with both waters), sometimes with a cost elsewhere. Loci inherit like real ones: recessive colour morphs, incompletely dominant leucism (one copy pale, two white), dominant marbling (two copies frail), a mutator gene, and hybrid vigour from mixed genes against the cost of inbreeding.
- **Lifespans** follow the real animals (compressed): an octopus or a jellyfish lives a few minutes, a snail about 7, a koi or a turtle over half an hour. Short-lived species lay more and mature fast; long-lived ones breed again and again.
- **Temperament and care:** each animal is born with a vigor (how slowly it ages) and a wanderlust (how readily it moves on). Every species likes certain plants or rocks nearby (clownfish anemones, frogs lily pads and duckweed, crabs rocks and coral, and so on). Comfortable animals age slower and rarely wander off, and so do well-fed ones. The hover card shows how comfortable an animal is and what it likes.
- **Hunger and growth:** animals burn energy and forage. Plants shed drifting plankton, and the food you drop is a treat that keeps them well fed for a while. Babies hatch small and grow.
- **Breeding:** well-fed adults near a mate lay eggs. Fish eggs stick to plants, snail eggs go on rocks, and frog spawn floats at the surface. Frog eggs hatch into tadpoles that turn into froglets.
- **Predators and prey:** hungry eels, snakes, octopuses and predatory wild fish hunt small fish, shrimp, tadpoles and babies, and prey flee. Frogs snap gnats and fireflies with their tongues. Hunger drives it: a predator that has just eaten is sated and lets prey swim by (longer after a bigger meal, and a meal is worth the size of what it caught). Past its hunger threshold (aggressive ones sooner) it hunts, and the hungrier it gets the further it looks, the harder it chases and the longer it keeps after one quarry. Starving, it takes bigger prey than usual, even the young of its own kind.
- **The eldritch:** a rare branch of evolution. About one birth in 3,000 carries *the mark* (touched), as do some young born in the abyss, near the drowned idol, a dark island or a corrupted plant, and fossils. A marked animal looks wrong from the start: veins of void open in its skin, windows onto a starry dark that crawl as it moves, wider at every stage. Over its life it changes, faster at night, in deep water, near the idol or the whale fall, and near the mythic: its eyes stop reflecting the light; then it is *Changed*, with new eyes along its flanks, feelers, and a wrongness in the water that unsettles its neighbours; then *Transcendent* (eldritch), with a crown of tentacles and a sigil that glows at night. Every other animal keeps a little distance from the marked, and each time one changes, the whole pond scatters from it for a moment. The changed and the transcendent take in lesser animals of their own kind (often when their kind is crowded), growing bigger, hardier and more deformed with each, up to six. Small animals circle a transcendent one as if listening, those too close swim as if something is wrong with them, and at night it can dream the mark into a neighbour, until about one animal in twelve is marked and the pond resists. In the shallows most marked animals die before they transcend. The mark passes to young (15% from one marked parent, 35% from two). From its card you can *feed the dream* or *bind it* (essence), and with corruption *swell in the void*, *offer* an unmarked animal to the dream, or have a transcendent one *Ascend*. The first of each species to transcend deepens the pond.
  - *The eldritch paths* (bought with corruption in the depths): the Veil of Stars (wider void, and hunters look through the marked), the Many Eyes (more corruption), the Hunger (they take in their own twice as often), the Deep Dream (the mark passes more readily; any animal can be offered), the Madness Chorus (wider madness), the Black Tide (the transcendent wear the pond deeper; the mythic rise twice as often) and the Drowned Crown (Ascension).
- **Coming and going:** old and starving animals die where they are (see remains), restless ones swim off the edge, and newcomers fade in from the edges to keep each species near its population.
- **Wild species:** a generator invents new fish species, each with its own body shape, tail and fin style, colour scheme, pattern, behaviour (schooling, depth, predator or not) and name, such as "Barred Azure Discus". New ones turn up now and then, or you can add one with the *Wild fish* button.
- **Moon, tides and surf:** the moon goes round every 8 days. Its phase sets how bright the nights are. It also sets the tides: every pond (except a pool) has a beach along one edge, and the tide floods and bares it twice a day. Spring tides at new and full moon move the water furthest; neap tides at the quarters barely move it. Freshwater ponds hardly have tides at all. The surf grows with spring tides and wind. Waves roll in as foam lines, shallow water is clearer, and water animals turn back before the waterline, while crabs, turtles, snails, starfish and frogs can cross the sand. The tidal stream pushes the current toward the beach and back. On a full-moon night the corals spawn: pink clouds drift up and the fish feast.
- **Ticker:** the bottom-left line shows one entry at a time, for as long as it takes to read. Important news (rares, new species, records, big surf, coral spawning) stays longer and jumps the queue. Routine news (eggs laid, animals growing up or old, departures) skips the ticker when it's busy but is always in the journal. A small `+N` shows what's waiting.
- **Journal:** each entry has a category (life, rare, hunts, comings and goings, sky and tide). Repeat events within about 40 seconds fold into one line with a count and a breakdown, e.g. "4 Tetras moved on (3 of old age, 1 hungry)" or "3 clutches of Koi hatched: 8 young, up to gen 3". It covers hatchings, eggs laid, animals growing up and growing old, record generations, arrivals, departures with reasons, catches, rare births, new species, weather, dawn, dusk, moonrise, high and low tide, big surf and coral spawning.
- **Surface and weather:** ripples come from food, frogs, duck wakes, rising bubbles and rain showers. Wind gusts push the current around. **Weather** toggles rain and wind. Clear spells run long; now and then comes a *glass day*: no wind, no surf, the tide barely stirring, and the water so clear you can see far down into the deep.
- **The heavens:** at night, sometimes, a meteor shower (and a star-stone may come down on the beach), an aurora (green and violet over the night), or a comet for a few nights (rare births come more often). Under a full moon, a blood moon: the night goes red and madness runs high. Rarely an eclipse puts the sun out at midday. And, very rarely (oftener the more the pond has given itself to the dark), the stars come right: everything eldritch runs faster, the mythic rise, and it rains blood. Blood rain also falls under a blood moon and over a pond steeped in corruption: the water reddens, and the marked drink it in. The sky panel says what's in the sky.
- **Music:** off until you turn it on (*Music* in the menu, or **N**; its level is in Scene). Two pieces, *Subaquatic Matrix* and *Subdued Progression* (both 90 bpm, both on D), cut into 4-bar phrases, each tagged with a mood from how loud, bright and busy it is: deep, glow, drift, rise or surge. Nothing plays for long: what happens in the pond cues a phrase in a mood that suits it (a mythic arrival or the pond deepening: eight bars of the full groove; the mark, the deep, an artifact: the low, dark pads; an aurora, a new species, a first: something bright; dawn and dusk, hunts, arrivals and births: shorter and quieter), faded in and out, well under the soundscape, with a minute or two of rest between. Phrases tend to carry on from the last, so the pieces unfold over an evening; the deeper you look, the more muffled they sound. Only the phrases it plays are downloaded (about 200–400 KB each).
- **Hover card:** hover an animal to see its name, species, stage, generation, age, energy and mood (hunting, fleeing, hungry, growing, moving on).

## How it works

- `js/core.js`: math and noise. `Chain` is the core idea from the original sketch. The head is
  steered. Each following joint keeps a fixed distance from the one ahead and may only bend a limited
  angle from it. `solveLimb` is two-bone IK. `bakeShader` turns a pattern function into a lookup table.
- `js/raster.js`: a small software rasterizer. Bodies are tapered tubes and ellipsoids drawn into a
  low-res height buffer. Each pixel's normal gives banded, Bayer-dithered lighting. Its height sets
  its drop-shadow offset and decides what occludes what, and outlines appear wherever a shape meets
  something lower. `compose` also applies caustics, the light tint and the glow exemption.
- `js/creatures.js`: the base classes and the original species. `Fish` swimmers sway their head
  around the heading, and the chain turns that into a travelling wave. `Walker` plants its feet and
  steps in alternating groups. Leg layout is per leg (`ang`, `sAng`, `bend`), and a creature can
  override `legBase` (crabs face sideways).
- `js/critters.js`: crab, stingray, frog (states: sit on pad, hop, swim), water snake, snail, and
  jellyfish (its tentacles are trailing chains).
- `js/wildfish.js`: the procedural species generator (body profile, tail, fins, palette ramps, patterns, names) and `WildFish`.
- `js/life.js`: genes and dyes, growth, eggs, tadpoles, gnats, predators and prey, migration, plankton, ripples, bubbles, weather, and the hover-card text.
- `js/wildlife.js`: clownfish, pufferfish, octopus, ducks, shrimp, dragonfly and firefly.
- `js/plants.js`: weeds, eelgrass, anemones, marimo, duckweed and lily pads. They react to
  passing animals and the current.
- `js/scene.js`: floor textures, water tints, scenery layout, food, and the background bake.
  Rocks are z-tested against animals.
- `js/main.js`: the loop, the day/night cycle, fireflies, options, tools, zoom and pan, input and the HUD.
- `js/sound.js`: the Web Audio soundscape, including a surf wash that swells as each wave arrives.
- `js/save.js`: serializing and restoring whole ponds, and the localStorage save slots. Animals and plants are built by `makeCreature` and `makePlant` under their own seeds, so a save stores each one's seed plus its changing state (genes, name, age and so on) rather than its shapes and colours.
- `js/link.js`: packs a pond into a link and back. The format stores only what can't be recomputed:
  - scenery regrows from the pond seed, and only the player's edits are stored;
  - an animal's seed follows from the pond seed and its number;
  - genes follow from the seed, or from the parents plus the seed;
  - names and lifespans follow from the seed.

  That leaves about 6 bytes per animal, so a 120-animal pond fits in about 1,200 characters. The bytes are deflated and written as base64url; v1 links still open.
- `js/sky.js`: days, the moon, tides, surf and coral spawning, plus the sky events in the journal.
- `js/game.js`: points, pearls and essence, rarity scoring, spawn prices and gene boosts, recycling, comfort, the family-tree records, the dawn income, and how many fireflies the score earns.
- `js/ecology.js`: difficulty per habitat, and the aggression and fresh/salt grids.
- `js/structures.js`: structures (shapes, auras, placing), islands raising the beach, the hatchery's idle breeding, plant life (growth, seeding, dying back), and the slow stains and plant growth around old things.
- `js/erosion.js`: erosion, tide pools, the depth map, and the depth tiers that grow the world.
- `js/deep.js`: sharks, deep-water species and the mythic ones, the evolution tree's species, and deep arrivals.
- `js/remains.js`: dying in place, remains and scavengers, fossils and ancient genes.
- `js/eldritch.js`: the eldritch branch: the mark's stages, its psychic effects, keeping away and scattering, taking in their own, corruption and the paths, how it's drawn, and the options on an animal's card.
- `js/coast.js`: the river, islands through the tides and seasons, litter, pollution and blights, what building out into the deep does, and scour.
- `js/gulls.js`: gulls. `js/traits.js`: the trait trees for animals, plants and structures (island raising and its two branches).
- `js/sand.js`: sandbars, the river delta, spits in the lee, plant beds trapping sand, and how sand looks on the floor.
- `js/tips.js`: the pixel-art tooltips (every `title` is rerouted into one) and what things in the pond say when you point at them.
- `js/story.js`: the narrator and the light/dark balance of madness. `js/lineage.js`: super spawns, paragons and keep safe. `js/depths.js`: light pools, trenches, the deep's tint by tier, the rise and fall of swimmers, and what deep islands cost. `js/heavens.js`: glass days, meteors, auroras, comets, blood moons, eclipses, the stars coming right and blood rain. `js/nature.js`: rage, blood in the water, the chase and the dice. `js/pollution.js`: wrecks sized by depth, the oil rig, slicks and the tar. `js/prehistoric.js`: the deep past and the tiers made up past it. `js/account.js`: signing in and keeping ponds in an account. `js/wander.js`: wanderers between ponds. `js/alien.js`: alien artifacts, parasites, their contagions, the evolved and the quarantine lamp. `js/bestiary.js`: the designed species (58 kinds from a catalogue of shapes, colours and extras, on three bodies: swimmers, crawlers and drifters). `js/guide.js`: the guide, written from the game's tables, with a line about each species. `js/music.js`: the opt-in music (phrases cued by the journal). `js/land.js`: the land's memory, formations, sinking bones, islands' characters, flora and joins. `js/isles.js`: islands growing up, their size and coastline drifting, what arises on them and what their cards can grow, and the life on them. `js/hinterland.js`: the land up the beach. `js/shore.js`: the beach's own animals. `js/mastery.js`: how traits built all the way show. `js/cycle.js`: detritus, scavengers, litter taken up, and the pond growing each dawn. `js/arrivals.js`: which kinds are known, each pond's odds, the pace of new kinds, lures, summons and the Wildlife window. `js/interact.js`: interactions between kinds, their signs, protect and cull, and the refuge. `tools/music_chop.py` cuts the source tracks (kept out of the repo) into `audio/music/` and `audio/music.json`: it finds the tempo and downbeats, splits them into 4-bar pieces, measures and tags each, and writes 8-bar MP3s with a short tail; `tools/music_analyze.py` shows a track's tempo, loudness and brightness over time.
- `js/abyss.js`: the four tiers past the abyss, with their species, structures, plants and foods. `js/quirks.js`: gifts, curses, quality grades, madness quirks and ills (a fifth gene stream). `js/hunters.js`: building up hunters. `js/habitats.js`: breeding habitats and keeping populations in check. `js/artifacts.js`: graded fossils and the artifacts.
- `js/net.js`: the client for the pond API: short links, syncing, the leaderboard.
- `js/hud.js`: the animal dock with its family trees, the census, the journal UI, the sky tracker, and the score panel. Icons are rendered at runtime by the pond's own renderer.
- `server/`: the pond API, plain Node 24 with its built-in SQLite and no packages. `POST /api/ponds` stores a pond under a new four-word id (from `words.js`) and returns a secret key; `PUT /api/ponds/:id` with that key updates it; `GET /api/ponds/:id` returns it; `GET /api/board` gives the top twenty, the high-score line and recent rare finds. `GET /api/neighbours?id=` gives the ponds either side of one along the shared beach (every pond active in the last month, in the order they were made, in a ring). Each pond counts its visitors (one view per address every six hours; a `?peek=1` fetch, used to draw a neighbour past the end of the beach, doesn't count), and the owner hears the count on update. Ponds are ranked by depth (fathoms from the reported erosion, computed the same way as the page). Scores and depth are capped by how fast they can plausibly grow, and finds are rebuilt from known species and trait names.
- Starfish live in `js/wildlife.js`; corals and urchins live in `js/plants.js`.

## Hosting

The `Dockerfile` builds a small nginx image that serves the site files. Configuration is in
`deploy/nginx.conf`: caching, gzip, security headers, a Content-Security-Policy, and 301 redirects from `www.pond.nz` and `pond.sardistic.com` to the canonical `pond.nz`. It serves four-word paths as `index.html` and proxies `/api/` (rate-limited) to the network alias `pond-api`, a service built from `server/Dockerfile` with a volume at `/data` for its SQLite file. The name is resolved per request, so the site keeps serving if the API is down.

```sh
docker build -t procedural-pond .
docker run --rm -p 8080:80 procedural-pond   # then open http://localhost:8080
```

Signing in is off unless the API has `DISCORD_CLIENT_ID` and `DISCORD_CLIENT_SECRET` in its environment (a Discord application's OAuth2 client, with `<site>/api/auth/discord/callback` registered as a redirect; `PUBLIC_URL` sets the site, `https://pond.nz` by default). The only scope asked for is `identify`. Sessions are random tokens in an HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax cookie on `/api`, stored hashed, for 60 days.

When you change a script or the stylesheet, bump the `?v=N` on its tag in `index.html`. When you
add a new top-level asset, list it in the `Dockerfile`.

`node tools/make-icons.js` regenerates the favicon and app icons by rendering a koi with the pond's
own renderer. SEO lives in `index.html` (description, canonical, Open Graph and Twitter cards,
JSON-LD) plus `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml` and `manifest.webmanifest`.
